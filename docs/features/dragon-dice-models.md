# Dragon dice models

Two included procedural designs extend the shared Dice Forge model and skin catalogs:

- **Hollow Dragon Silver** (`skin-hollow-dragon`, `model-dragon-hollow`): open metal faces with sculpted dragon bodies, horns, wing membranes/ribs, curled tails and gold lettering.
- **Emerald Dragon Eye** (`skin-dragon-eye`, `model-dragon-eye`): raised shield scales over a dark metal core, green enamel lettering and a convex emerald eye on the D20's 20 face.

Choose a complete preset under **Dice Skin**, or use **Shape** to apply either model to existing colors. Both support D4, D6, D8, D10, D12, D20 and percentile dice. The eye replaces the visible 20; it retains the authoritative value 20. D4 numbers remain grouped at the four corners.

The models are original procedural interpretations of the supplied references. They are generated by `engine/models/dragon-*.ts` and `sculpt-mesh.builder.ts`, rather than downloaded GLB assets. The existing convex geometry remains the collision/result proxy. Decoration is batched into one metal mesh per die; the scale set adds one core mesh and the D20 adds one eye lens. No additional lights or per-frame ornament animation are allocated. Face lettering uses alpha-tested textures lifted above the relief; it is not an individually extruded numeral mesh.

Owned ornament materials and the iris texture are disposed with the die. Geometry is deterministic and bounded to fewer than 70,000 extra vertices per die. Saved presets retain the catalog model/skin IDs.

The API's curated skins and included unlock lists carry both IDs, so authoritative roll responses preserve the chosen dragon skin instead of falling back to Obsidian.

Validation: `node scripts/test_dice_dragons.cjs` checks all 14 type/design combinations, topology, saved IDs, the D20-only lens and the vertex budget. `runDragonBrowserRegression(scene)` additionally checks transparent openings, preserved 20 mapping, mesh count and GPU resource cleanup. Existing geometry and motion regressions cover numbering, D4 apex values and stable result-aligned rolls. Visual inspection uses the actual Babylon factory in a local browser.
